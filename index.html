September 11, 2001

A single morning that changed how the world understood safety, war, and grief. This page records what happened, where, and what followed.

Overview

Four flights, one morning

On the morning of September 11, 2001, nineteen hijackers affiliated with the terrorist group al-Qaeda took control of four commercial airplanes departing from airports on the American East Coast. Two aircraft were flown into the twin towers of the World Trade Center in Lower Manhattan. A third struck the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia. The fourth crashed into a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, after passengers and crew attempted to retake control of the cockpit.

The attacks killed nearly 3,000 people in the span of about ninety minutes and remain the deadliest act of terrorism in modern history, as well as the single deadliest incident for firefighters and law enforcement officers in United States history.

Timeline

The morning, hour by hour

All times below are Eastern Daylight Time, as reported by the 9/11 Commission's investigation into the day's events.

7:59AM

Flight 11 departs Boston

American Airlines Flight 11, bound for Los Angeles, leaves Logan International Airport with 92 people aboard. It is hijacked shortly after takeoff.

8:46AM

North Tower struck

Flight 11 crashes into the North Tower of the World Trade Center, between the 93rd and 99th floors. Early reports describe it as a possible accident.

9:03AM

South Tower struck

United Airlines Flight 175 hits the South Tower, live on national television. It becomes immediately clear the crashes are a deliberate attack.

9:37AM

The Pentagon struck

American Airlines Flight 77 crashes into the western side of the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia, killing everyone aboard and 125 people inside the building.

9:59AM

South Tower collapses

Fifty-six minutes after impact, the South Tower falls, sending debris and dust through Lower Manhattan.

10:03AM

Flight 93 crashes near Shanksville

Having learned of the other attacks by phone, passengers and crew on United Airlines Flight 93 storm the cockpit. The plane goes down in a field in Somerset County, Pennsylvania, short of its intended target in Washington, D.C.

10:28AM

North Tower collapses

The North Tower falls roughly 102 minutes after being struck, completing the destruction of the World Trade Center complex.

Where it happened

Three sites, one day

World Trade Center

Lower Manhattan, New York City. Both towers collapsed within two hours of impact, along with several surrounding buildings. 2,753 people were killed at this site, including 343 New York City firefighters and 71 law enforcement officers.

The Pentagon

Arlington, Virginia, headquarters of the U.S. Department of Defense. The impact and resulting fire caused a partial collapse of one section of the building. 184 people were killed.

Shanksville, Pennsylvania

An open field in Somerset County. All 40 passengers and crew aboard Flight 93 were killed. The plane's intended target is believed to have been the U.S. Capitol or the White House.

Aftermath

What followed

The attacks reshaped American foreign and domestic policy within weeks. The United States and its allies launched the invasion of Afghanistan in October 2001, beginning what became a two-decade military engagement. Congress passed the USA PATRIOT Act, expanding government surveillance powers, and in 2002 established the Department of Homeland Security, consolidating dozens of federal agencies under a single cabinet department.

Air travel changed permanently. The Transportation Security Administration was created to federalize airport screening, and cockpit doors on commercial aircraft were reinforced worldwide. Recovery and cleanup at the World Trade Center site continued for months; the last steel column was removed in May 2002.

One World Trade Center, also known as the Freedom Tower, was completed on the site in 2014, alongside the National September 11 Memorial & Museum.

Remembrance

Never forget

Two reflecting pools now sit within the footprints of the fallen towers, each edged in bronze parapets inscribed with the names of everyone who died in the attacks, and in the 1993 World Trade Center bombing.

September 11 is observed in the United States as Patriot Day. Ceremonies are held each year at all three sites, marked by moments of silence at the exact times the towers were struck and fell, and at the times Flight 77 and Flight 93 went down.
